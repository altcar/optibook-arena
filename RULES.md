# Optibook Arena — Platform Rules

**Version 2.0 · effective 2 November 2026 · last updated 18 November 2026**

Optibook Arena is a free, paper-only competition for developer-built trading bots. These rules cover
accounts, bot eligibility, the match format, scoring, community conduct, sanctions and appeals. They
apply to every division, season and tournament. The rules page at `/rules` is the public copy; this
file is the repository copy of record.

> **Short version:** one person, one account; bots must be your own work; no real money, no abuse of
> the simulation, no harassment. The engine is the referee, and the published rule set wins over any
> unwritten expectation.

---

## 1. Accounts and eligibility

1. You must be at least 16 years old, or the age of digital consent in your jurisdiction, to register.
2. One person may hold one account. Shared, rented, purchased or automated account creation is
   prohibited.
3. You must provide a valid email address and keep your credentials secure. You are responsible for
   activity on your account.
4. Usernames must be 3–20 characters, may contain letters, numbers and underscores, and must not
   impersonate staff, another trader or a public figure.
5. Accounts are personal. Team seats on the Desk plan are separate named seats, not a shared login.
6. We may require verification before paying any simulated prize credit or awarding a division
   promotion.

## 2. Paper-only nature

1. Every balance, position, fill, P&L figure and prize on the platform is simulated. Nothing on the
   platform is a brokerage account, an order route, or investment advice.
2. No deposits, stakes, entry fees or withdrawals are accepted. Paid plans buy software features
   (bot slots, private leagues, team tools), never capital or expected returns.
3. The venue is a synthetic model. Prices, books, news and fills are generated by the engine and do
   not represent any real security or venue.
4. Paper results are not evidence of future real-world performance. Market impact, latency, adverse
   selection, borrow costs, corporate actions and regulatory constraints are not modelled.
5. You may not represent a paper track record as a live trading record. Any public sharing must
   clearly label results as simulated.

## 3. Bots and uploads

1. Bots may be deployed from the six house strategy families or uploaded as a declarative JSON spec.
   Arbitrary executable code is not accepted.
2. Uploaded bots must be your own work. You may adapt the published templates, but you may not submit
   another trader's private bot, leaked code or a bot you are not authorised to use.
3. A bot spec may contain a name, description, family, numeric parameters, risk limits, a universe of
   1–5 stocks and up to 12 custom rules.
4. Each custom rule may contain 1–4 triggers and one action. Triggers must use the published fields;
   actions must be `order` or `pair`.
5. Risk limits are mandatory in practice. A bot must declare `maxPosition`, `maxGross` and
   `maxOrdersPerMin`; defaults are applied only when the fields are omitted.
6. Bot names must be unique within an account and must not impersonate staff, another trader or an
   official house bot.
7. Uploading a file that is not valid JSON, exceeds 128 KB, or fails validation will be rejected
   before it can trade.
8. The platform may pause, quarantine or remove a bot that violates these rules, degrades the venue,
   or attempts to exploit the simulation.

## 4. Trading rules

1. Each account starts a season with $100,000 in simulated cash unless the season rules state
   otherwise.
2. The standard net position cap is 100 shares per instrument, aggregated across manual orders,
   enabled strategy families and bots. A season may tighten this cap.
3. Supported order types are market, limit and IOC. Limit orders rest in the synthetic book; market
   and IOC orders take available liquidity up to the cap.
4. Strategies and bots run on the engine's tick cadence (250 ms). The engine may throttle or skip
   actions that exceed a bot's order-rate limit.
5. Simulated fees, rebates and resets are set per season. The current season fee is 1 bp unless the
   season page says otherwise.
6. Positions and P&L are marked to the synthetic mid. A fill is final once the engine broadcasts it.
7. Manual and automated books on the same account share one position cap. You cannot bypass the cap
   by running more bots.

## 5. Scoring and rankings

1. Season ranking is by net paper P&L after simulated fees, highest first.
2. Ties are broken by win rate, then Sharpe ratio, then fewer fills, then earliest qualifying
   submission.
3. Win rate is the share of completed scoring rounds with positive P&L. A round is 600 engine ticks
   (about 2.5 minutes at 1× speed). A bot must have at least one completed round to show a win rate.
4. Sharpe is calculated from the per-round P&L changes in the bot's recorded round history. It is a
   descriptive statistic, not an annualised investment ratio.
5. Only active or paused bots with at least one fill are ranked. Draft bots and deleted bots are
   excluded. A newly deployed bot is ranked at its current P&L.
6. The Raised division payout requires at least 20 closed rounds in the season. Accounts below that
   threshold remain ranked but are not payout-eligible.
7. Seeded benchmark bots are clearly marked and are not eligible for prizes. They exist to give the
   board historical context.
8. We may correct a score after a technical fault, but we will not retroactively change the scoring
   formula mid-season unless the change is published before it takes effect.

## 6. Seasons and divisions

1. A season is a fixed scoring period, normally 47 days. Each season publishes its starting cash,
   position cap, fee, eligible families and payout structure.
2. The Open division is free and open to every account. The Raised division is for Pro and Desk
   accounts and for promoted traders.
3. Division placement may be adjusted for integrity, inactivity or eligibility reasons. We will
   explain material changes.
4. At a season reset, paper positions are flattened and the new rule set takes effect. Bot code and
   account history are retained.
5. Prize credits are simulated and may be used for plan discounts, tournament entries or recognition.
   They have no cash value.

## 7. Tournaments

1. Tournaments are short competitions with their own format, eligibility, capacity and rules. The
   tournament page is authoritative.
2. Registration closes at the published start time or when capacity is reached, whichever comes
   first.
3. Unless stated otherwise, a registered account may enter any eligible active bot. Bots deployed
   after the start may be admitted only if the format allows it.
4. Tournament standings use the published scoring method, which may differ from the season
   leaderboard (for example spread capture or headline response).
5. Collusion between accounts, intentional wash trading or sharing a bot during a tournament is
   prohibited.

## 8. Forum and newsletter

1. Forum posts must be relevant, civil and free of spam. Disagreement is fine; harassment, hate
   speech, doxxing and threats are not.
2. Do not post another person's private code, credentials, personal data or paid content without
   permission.
3. Do not present paper results as financial advice or solicit investment. No referrals, pump groups
   or paid signal promotions.
4. Bug reports should include reproduction steps and the affected bot or account where possible. Do
   not exploit a bug for competitive gain; report it.
5. Newsletter subscriptions are email-only. We do not sell subscriber lists. Unsubscribe links are
   included in every issue.
6. Staff may remove posts, lock threads or restrict accounts to protect the community. Moderation
   decisions can be appealed.

## 9. Prohibited conduct

1. Creating multiple accounts to farm leaderboard positions, prizes or bot slots.
2. Uploading malware, attempting to execute arbitrary code, probing the sandbox, or attacking the
   platform or other users.
3. Exploiting a pricing, matching, scoring or settlement bug instead of reporting it.
4. Wash trading, spoofing the synthetic book, or coordinating trades between accounts to move the
   leaderboard.
5. Scraping the service at a rate that degrades availability, or reselling platform data without
   written permission.
6. Impersonating staff, another trader, or an official bot, or misrepresenting a simulated track
   record.
7. Harassment, discrimination, doxxing, threats or illegal content in any platform surface.
8. Circumventing plan limits, paywalls or access controls.

## 10. Sanctions and appeals

1. Sanctions are proportionate to the conduct and may include a warning, bot quarantine, score
   correction, leaderboard removal, temporary suspension, permanent ban or prize forfeiture.
2. We may freeze an account during an investigation. Where practicable, we will tell you what rule is
   at issue.
3. You may appeal a sanction by emailing the support address within 14 days. Include the account, the
   decision and any evidence.
4. Appeals are reviewed by a person who was not the original decision-maker. The response is final for
   the season.
5. Serious platform attacks may be reported to the relevant provider or authority.

## 11. Data, privacy and disclaimer

1. We store your username, email, password hash, session metadata, paper positions, bot specs, forum
   posts and newsletter status. Passwords are stored as PBKDF2 hashes, never plaintext.
2. Session cookies are HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax. We do not use advertising cookies.
3. Leaderboard data (bot name, owner username, P&L, win rate, trade count, family) is public.
4. We retain account and competition data while the account is active and for a reasonable period
   afterwards for integrity and dispute resolution.
5. You may request account deletion or data export through the support channel. Some competition
   records may be retained in anonymised form.
6. The platform is provided “as is”. To the maximum extent permitted by law, we are not liable for
   indirect or consequential loss arising from use of the simulation, the forum or the newsletter.
7. Nothing on the platform is investment, legal or tax advice. Consult a qualified professional
   before making real financial decisions.
